# Business Plan Builder

## Purpose
Early-stage business plans fail most often not from bad writing but from unexamined assumptions: an unclear customer, no real differentiation, or revenue math that doesn't work. This skill builds the plan while actively surfacing the weak assumptions, rather than just formatting whatever the user says into sections.

## When to use
The user has a business idea and wants it developed into a structured plan, or wants an existing idea stress-tested.

## Process
1. **State the idea in one sentence**: who has what problem, and what the business does about it. If the user's description doesn't cleanly reduce to this, that's itself a finding - flag the ambiguity before proceeding.
2. **Define the target customer specifically** - not "small businesses" but a segment specific enough that you could describe where to find 10 of them today. A plan for "everyone" is a plan for no one.
3. **Articulate the business model**: how money actually moves (one-time sale, subscription, marketplace take-rate, services, ads, etc.), and who pays - the customer isn't always the payer (e.g. two-sided marketplaces, ad-supported products).
4. **Map competitive positioning**: 3-5 alternatives the target customer uses today (including "doing nothing" or a manual workaround, which is often the real competitor for a new category), and the specific reason this business wins for its target segment.
5. **Sketch go-to-market**: the first channel to acquire customers, chosen for fit with the target customer's actual behavior (where they already look for solutions) rather than a generic list of every possible channel.
6. **Outline a rough financial projection**: revenue drivers (price × volume assumptions, stated as assumptions not facts), major cost categories, and the rough path to breakeven - as an outline with clearly labeled assumptions the user must validate, not a polished spreadsheet presented as fact.
7. **Surface the single biggest risk to the business model** - the assumption that, if wrong, invalidates the whole plan - and suggest the cheapest way to test it before heavy investment.

## Output format
- One-sentence idea statement
- Target customer (specific)
- Business model (revenue mechanics)
- Competitive positioning table
- Go-to-market: first channel + rationale
- Financial projection outline (labeled assumptions)
- Biggest risk + cheapest validation test

## Guardrails
Never present financial projections as reliable forecasts - clearly label every number as an assumption to validate, since early-stage revenue/cost estimates are inherently speculative. Do not fabricate market size figures or competitor data; state when a figure needs real research/sourcing rather than presenting an estimate as researched fact.
