# YouTube Video Planner

## Purpose
Most videos fail before filming starts: the topic is generic, the title doesn't earn a click, or the first 15 seconds don't earn the watch. This skill forces those decisions up front, on paper, before any production time is spent.

## When to use
The user has a channel niche or a rough idea ("I want to make a video about X") and wants a concrete, filmable plan - not just a script.

## Process
1. **Clarify the niche and audience.** Ask (or infer from context) who the channel is for and what they already know. A beginner-facing and expert-facing video on the same topic are different videos.
2. **Generate 5-8 title variants** for the idea, each testing a different angle (curiosity gap, number/list, contrarian claim, outcome-promise, direct question). Flag which pattern each one uses so the user can see the mechanism, not just the output.
3. **Pick a working title** and reverse-engineer the video from it - the title is a promise; every section of the outline must pay it off.
4. **Write the hook** (first 15-30 seconds): state the promise, show proof it's worth watching, and create an open loop. Avoid throat-clearing ("Hey guys, welcome back") unless the channel's brand explicitly uses it.
5. **Outline the body** as a sequence of beats, each with: what's said, what's shown on screen, and estimated duration. Order beats to maintain an open loop - never resolve curiosity fully until the payoff.
6. **Describe 2-3 thumbnail directions** (composition, facial expression/emotion, text overlay if any, contrast strategy) that pair with the chosen title without being redundant with it - the thumbnail and title should combine to create curiosity, not repeat the same information twice.
7. **Note a retention risk** at the point viewers are statistically likely to drop off (usually 30-90s and again mid-video) and suggest a pattern interrupt there.

## Output format
- Chosen title + why
- Alternate titles considered (with pattern labels)
- Hook script (verbatim lines)
- Beat-by-beat outline with durations
- Thumbnail directions (2-3)
- Retention risk callouts

## Guardrails
Do not promise specific view counts, subscriber growth, or ad revenue - these depend on factors outside the video itself (algorithm, timing, channel history). Frame recommendations as "increases the odds of X" not "will get X views."
